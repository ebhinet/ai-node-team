# Sample Call Transcript — Marigold Wellness Studio
_Date: 2026-03-19_
_Interviewer: EB (Web Consultant)_
_Client: Sara Okonkwo, Owner — Marigold Wellness Studio_
_Format: Discovery call, ~45 minutes_

---

**EB:** Thanks for making time today, Sara. Let's start simple — tell me about Marigold in your own words.

**Sara:** Sure. So Marigold has been open for about three years. We're a boutique wellness studio — we do yoga, Pilates, and sound bath sessions. We're in a pretty small town, Millbrook, maybe 12,000 people. But we pull clients from three or four surrounding towns too. We have a really loyal base, maybe 80 or 90 regular clients, and we're trying to grow that.

**EB:** What's prompting the website now? Do you have something existing or are we starting from scratch?

**Sara:** We have something but it's honestly embarrassing. It was built by a friend of a friend like five years ago. It doesn't work on phones, the class schedule is just a PDF that I have to email her to update, and there's no way to book online. People literally call me or text me to book. I'm spending probably two hours a week just on booking coordination.

**EB:** That's a real pain point. So booking is a priority.

**Sara:** The biggest one, yes. If I could just wake up and see bookings in an inbox instead of managing a text thread, that would change my week.

**EB:** Makes sense. What else does the site need to do besides handle bookings?

**Sara:** I want people to be able to see the class schedule — what's happening when, who's teaching. We have three instructors besides me. And I'd love a way to sell gift cards. That's huge for us around the holidays. Oh, and I started doing a little corporate wellness thing — going to offices and doing chair yoga and guided breathwork. That's new and I want to have a page for it.

**EB:** Got it. So: online booking, class schedule, gift cards, and a corporate wellness section. Anything else on the list?

**Sara:** A contact form, obviously. And I want an email capture — like a pop-up or something — for people who aren't ready to book but want to follow along. We have a newsletter but our list is tiny.

**EB:** Who's your typical client? Paint me a picture.

**Sara:** Mostly women, 30 to 55. A lot of them are working professionals, some are moms with kids in school. They're health-conscious but not hardcore fitness people — like they're not going to a CrossFit gym. They want something that feels like a sanctuary. Calm, intentional. And they're busy, so they want things to be easy. Easy to find, easy to book, no friction.

**EB:** Is there a secondary audience you're thinking about — like the corporate wellness side?

**Sara:** Yeah, the corporate audience is different. More decision-maker type — HR managers, office managers. I'd probably want that section of the site to feel a little more professional, maybe a little less "spa-y." But I haven't fully thought that through.

**EB:** What's the main thing you want someone to do when they land on the site?

**Sara:** Book a class. That's it. I want that to be so obvious that nobody misses it.

**EB:** How are people finding you now, mostly?

**Sara:** Word of mouth, mostly. Instagram a little bit. We don't do much paid advertising. A few people have said they found us on Google but I don't know how strong that is.

**EB:** Do you have a booking system in mind, or is that part of what you need help figuring out?

**Sara:** I've looked at Mindbody and I've looked at something called Pike13. I kind of prefer Pike13 because it's simpler and less expensive, but I'm not locked in. I just need it to embed on the site somehow.

**EB:** That's doable. What's your content situation — do you have good photos of the studio?

**Sara:** We had a photographer come in last fall. I have maybe 40 or 50 photos. Some are really beautiful — the morning light in the main studio is stunning. I just haven't had a place to put them.

**EB:** Great. Do you write? Is a blog something you'd actually maintain or is it a "nice to have" that'll go stale?

**Sara:** Honest answer? It'll go stale. I've tried twice and I lose steam after a month. So I'd say no blog — or at least not on the main site.

**EB:** Good self-awareness, we'll skip it. What's the vibe you're going for visually?

**Sara:** I want it to feel calm and beautiful. Not clinical. Not trendy — like I don't want it to look like every other wellness brand that uses the same sage green and beige palette. I want something that feels a little more distinctive. Warm. Like you feel welcomed.

**EB:** Do you have any sites you've seen that you like? Doesn't have to be wellness.

**Sara:** Hmm. I really like... I think it's called Othership? They're a breathwork and sauna place in Toronto. Their site feels elevated but still warm. And I saw a ceramics studio once — I can't remember the name — but it had this really earthy, handcrafted feel. Not overly designed. I like that.

**EB:** What would you hate to see?

**Sara:** Anything that feels corporate or cold. Too many stock photos of women in white rooms smiling at cameras. And I hate busy layouts — like when there's too much happening and you don't know where to look.

**EB:** Colors or fonts — any direction there?

**Sara:** Not really? I trust you on that. I know I want warm, not cool. And nothing too thin and wispy — some wellness brands use these barely-there fonts and it feels fragile. I want something that feels grounded.

**EB:** Budget and timeline — where are you?

**Sara:** I want to launch before our summer session in late June. So... end of May would be ideal, giving me a few weeks buffer. Budget I've set aside around $3,500 to $4,500 all in.

**EB:** That's workable for what you're describing. Last question — who else is involved on your end? Are you the decision-maker or do I need to loop in anyone?

**Sara:** Just me. My husband helps with the books but this is fully my call.

---
_End of transcript._
