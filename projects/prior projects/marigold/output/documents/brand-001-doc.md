## Color Palette

### Primary Palette

| Name | Hex | Usage | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marigold Gold | `#D4942A` | Primary brand color — CTAs, logo accent, section highlights | Drawn directly from the marigold flower. A deep, saturated gold that reads warm without tipping into generic "wellness beige." Grounded and confident — not pastel, not precious. |
| Terracotta Clay | `#B5654A` | Secondary brand color — backgrounds, supporting elements, hover states | An earthy red-brown that evokes fired clay and sun-warmed earth. Pairs with the gold to create depth without competing. Avoids the sage-green/beige trap Sara flagged as a design anti-pattern. |
| Deep Umber | `#3C2415` | Text, headings, high-contrast elements | A rich dark brown that replaces pure black. Warmer and more inviting than black while maintaining strong readability. Grounds the palette without coldness. |

### Secondary / Accent Palette

| Name | Hex | Usage | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm Cream | `#F5EDE3` | Page backgrounds, card backgrounds, breathing room | A warm off-white with a slight golden undertone. Provides the lightness the palette needs without introducing coolness. Not stark white — nothing clinical. |
| Dried Sage | `#8A9A7B` | Sparse accent — testimonial cards, dividers, subtle greenery nods | A muted, dusty sage used sparingly. This is NOT a primary green — it appears only as a quiet accent to hint at the natural world. Used with restraint to avoid the generic wellness palette Sara wants to avoid. |

### Palette Relationship
The palette is anchored by two warm tones (Marigold Gold and Terracotta Clay) against a dark-warm neutral (Deep Umber) and a light-warm neutral (Warm Cream). The sage enters only at moments of rest — pull quotes, subtle dividers, occasional background tints. The overall impression is a sun-warmed pottery studio, not a spa catalog.

## Typography

### Heading Typeface: DM Serif Display
- **Source:** Google Fonts (free)
- **Weight used:** Regular (400)
- **Character:** A high-contrast serif with sturdy, rounded terminals. It reads as grounded and established — not delicate, not thin, not wispy. The strokes have warmth and substance.
- **Why this face:** Sara specified grounded typography — nothing thin or wispy. DM Serif Display has the heft and warmth that matches the earthy palette. Its rounded forms echo the organic quality of the marigold flower without being decorative or novelty. It reads well at large sizes (hero headings, section titles) and carries enough personality to anchor the brand.

### Body Typeface: Source Sans 3
- **Source:** Google Fonts (free)
- **Weights used:** Regular (400), Semibold (600)
- **Character:** A humanist sans-serif with open letterforms, generous x-height, and warm proportions. Highly legible across screen sizes.
- **Why this face:** Source Sans 3 is functional and warm without being clinical. Its humanist construction complements the serif heading face — the pairing creates a clear hierarchy (serif = personality, sans = clarity) without tension. The open letterforms ensure readability on mobile, which is critical given Sara's current site is not mobile-friendly. Semibold weight handles subheadings, labels, and CTAs without needing a third typeface.

### Pairing Rationale
The DM Serif Display / Source Sans 3 pairing creates a clear visual hierarchy: headings carry the brand's personality (warm, grounded, established), while body text stays clean and readable. The contrast in classification (serif vs. sans) makes the hierarchy unmistakable, and the shared warmth in both faces keeps the tone consistent. Neither face trends toward thin, geometric, or minimal — both have substance.

## Logo Concept

### Form
A stylized marigold flower rendered in a semi-geometric style — not a literal botanical illustration, but a simplified, structured interpretation that retains the layered-petal character of a marigold bloom. The flower is viewed from above (plan view), showing the characteristic dense, radial petal arrangement.

### Composition
- **Logomark:** The marigold bloom sits above or to the left of the wordmark. It is sized to work as a standalone icon (favicon, social avatar, class check-in screen) while remaining balanced alongside the text.
- **Wordmark:** "MARIGOLD" set in DM Serif Display, tracked out slightly (+30-50 tracking). Below it, "WELLNESS STUDIO" in Source Sans 3 Semibold, smaller, with wider tracking (+80-120) to match the width of the primary word. The two lines of text are optionally separated by a thin horizontal rule in Marigold Gold.
- **Color application:** The marigold bloom uses Marigold Gold (`#D4942A`) as its primary fill, with Terracotta Clay (`#B5654A`) on inner petal layers to create depth. The wordmark uses Deep Umber (`#3C2415`). On dark backgrounds, the wordmark reverses to Warm Cream (`#F5EDE3`) while the bloom retains its gold/terracotta coloring.

### Symbolism
The marigold flower is the direct, obvious choice — and that is intentional. Sara named her studio after this flower. The logo should make that connection immediate and unmistakable, not abstract it away. The layered petals suggest depth, care, and the unfolding nature of a wellness practice. The plan-view (top-down) perspective presents the flower as open and welcoming — a direct visual metaphor for the studio's role as a space people come into.

### Execution Detail
The petals should be constructed from overlapping rounded shapes — not perfectly symmetric, but structured enough to feel intentional. Think hand-drawn precision rather than mathematical perfection. Each petal layer (3-4 layers from center to edge) should be distinguishable, creating a sense of dimensionality even in flat color. The center of the bloom can use a slightly darker value of Terracotta Clay or a small circle of Deep Umber to anchor the form.

A graphic designer executing this concept should produce it as a vector (SVG/AI) with clean paths. The petal count per layer should be 8-12 (marigolds have dense petal arrangements — fewer than 8 reads as a generic flower; more than 12 becomes visually cluttered at small sizes).

## Imagery Direction

### Photography Style

**Mood:** Warm, grounded, lived-in. The studio should feel like a place people actually practice in — not a catalog set. Think morning light through a window, a mat with slight wear, hands in a mudra with real skin texture.

**Lighting:** Natural light is the dominant source. Warm-toned — golden hour, morning sun, soft window light. No cool fluorescent or harsh flash lighting. Shadows are soft but present — not flat, not overlit. The light should feel directional, creating gentle contrast and depth.

**Color Treatment:** Images should lean warm in post-processing. Slightly desaturated greens, boosted warm tones (golds, ambers, terracottas). Not a heavy filter — just a consistent warm shift that makes stock images feel cohesive with the brand palette. Blacks should be lifted slightly (matte look) to maintain warmth.

**Subject Matter:**
- Close-up, detail-oriented shots: hands, bare feet on a mat, a singing bowl's rim, folded towels, a candle flame, dried flowers
- Practice-in-progress: mid-pose shots from angles that show the movement, not the person's face (avoids the "woman smiling at camera" anti-pattern Sara flagged)
- Studio environment: empty studio with warm light, props arranged naturally, texture shots of wood floors, woven blankets, ceramic vessels
- Community: small groups in practice, shot from behind or the side — emphasizing togetherness, not individual performance

**Composition Principles:**
- Favor close crops and detail shots over wide establishing shots
- Negative space is encouraged — let compositions breathe
- Off-center subjects (rule of thirds, not centered-and-symmetrical)
- Depth of field: shallow to medium — keep backgrounds soft to maintain the warm, intimate feeling

### What to Avoid
These anti-patterns come directly from the intake record and must be enforced:

- **No women in white rooms smiling at cameras.** This is the single most flagged anti-pattern. If a person's face is visible, they should be in mid-practice, looking away, eyes closed, or in candid conversation — never posed toward the lens.
- **No busy layouts or cluttered imagery.** One clear subject per image. No collages, no overlapping elements, no stock-photo "lifestyle grids."
- **No cold or corporate imagery.** No glass offices, no steel-and-white interiors, no clinical studio shots. (Exception: the corporate wellness section may use slightly more structured imagery, but it should still lean warm — think a bright conference room with plants, not a sterile boardroom.)
- **No generic sage-green/beige aesthetic.** The earthy direction does not mean "neutral and safe." Images should have color — the marigold gold and terracotta clay should feel present in the image selection, whether through warm lighting, warm-toned environments, or actual objects in those colors.

### Illustration Style (If Used)
If spot illustrations are introduced (section dividers, icons, decorative elements), they should be:
- Simple line drawings with a slight hand-drawn quality
- Rendered in Deep Umber or Terracotta Clay on Warm Cream backgrounds
- Botanical or geometric motifs — marigold petals, simple leaf forms, radial patterns
- Never cartoon-like, never overly detailed, never clipart
