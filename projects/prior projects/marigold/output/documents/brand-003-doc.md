## Color Palette

### Primary Palette

| Color | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Saffron Ink | `#C47B2B` | Primary brand color — headings, logo mark, CTA buttons. A deep saffron gold that reads as hand-mixed pigment rather than digital yellow. Evokes marigold petals dried and pressed, not bright and screaming. |
| Charred Walnut | `#3B2F2A` | Primary text, navigation, footer backgrounds. A near-black brown with visible warmth — the color of burnt wood or walnut hull ink. Avoids the coldness of pure black. |
| Parchment | `#F4EDE4` | Page backgrounds, card surfaces, breathing space. An unbleached paper tone — not stark white, not beige. Carries the feeling of handmade paper with slight warmth. |
| Lichen Green | `#7A8B6F` | Secondary accent — section dividers, icon accents, hover states. A muted, dusty green pulled from moss on stone rather than a plant nursery shelf. Deliberately avoids the overused "wellness sage green" by pushing toward grey-green territory. |
| Dried Rose | `#B8827A` | Tertiary accent — highlights, pull quotes, decorative elements. A clay-fired pink with brown undertones — terracotta that has been weathered by rain. |

### Secondary / Accent Palette

| Color | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Raw Linen | `#E8DFD0` | Subtle background variation, alternating sections, card borders. A half-step darker than Parchment for depth without contrast. |
| Lamp Black | `#1E1A17` | Sparse use only — footer text on dark backgrounds, strong emphasis moments. True darkness when Charred Walnut isn't heavy enough. |
| Marigold Wash | `#E6A84D` | Sparingly — hover highlights on Saffron Ink elements, active states. A lighter, more translucent version of the primary gold, as if the pigment were thinned with water. |

### Palette Rationale

This palette is built around the idea of natural pigments — colors that could be mixed from plant matter, clay, and mineral. Every tone is slightly desaturated and carries visible warmth, as if hand-ground and applied with a brush rather than selected from a digital color picker. The Saffron Ink primary ties directly to the marigold flower but rendered as a pigment, not a photograph. Lichen Green was chosen specifically to avoid the generic sage/eucalyptus green that saturates wellness branding — it sits greyer and wilder. Dried Rose provides warmth without reading as feminine pink; it is earthy and tactile.

Sara's stated preference for "warm, calm, grounded, distinctive" is met through the desaturated warmth across all five primaries. Her anti-pattern of "generic sage-green/beige" is addressed by the deliberate avoidance of clean, trendy greens and the use of Parchment (warm paper, not beige) as the neutral base. The palette should feel like it belongs in a ceramics studio or a bookbinder's workshop — not a day spa.

## Typography

### Heading Typeface: Caveat

**Source:** Google Fonts (free)
**Classification:** Handwritten / semi-cursive

Caveat is a handwritten typeface with genuine pen-stroke character. Its letterforms feel like someone actually wrote them — not a perfected script font, but a confident, relaxed hand. The slight bounce and irregularity in the baseline gives it the spontaneous quality of a hand-lettered studio sign. At display sizes (H1, H2), it reads as personal and intentional.

Caveat is used exclusively for primary headings (H1) and feature callouts. It should not be overused — its power comes from contrast against the body type.

### Body Typeface: Source Serif 4

**Source:** Google Fonts (free)
**Classification:** Serif / transitional

Source Serif 4 is a workhorse serif with excellent readability at body sizes. Its slightly rounded terminals and moderate contrast give it warmth without stiffness — it reads as approachable and literate rather than formal or institutional. The serif construction provides grounding and substance that anchors the handwritten display face above it.

For secondary headings (H2, H3), Source Serif 4 in semibold or bold provides structure without reaching for a third typeface. This keeps the system tight and coherent.

### Pairing Rationale

The Caveat + Source Serif 4 combination creates a deliberate tension: the hand-drawn headings say "this was made by a person," while the well-set body text says "this person is serious and trustworthy." This pairing solves the core challenge of handcrafted branding — being artisanal without looking amateur. The handwritten face provides personality and distinctiveness; the serif body provides credibility and readability.

This pairing is distinctly different from Option A (warm/earthy — likely warmer, rounder type choices) and Option B (elevated/modern — likely clean sans-serifs or refined serif pairings). The handwritten display face is the clearest typographic signal that this is the handcrafted direction.

Typography sizing should feel generous. Line heights should be open (1.6-1.7 for body text). Letter spacing on headings should remain at default or slightly loose — never tight-tracked, which would undercut the handmade quality.

## Logo Concept

### Form

A hand-drawn marigold flower rendered in a loose, illustrative line style — as if sketched with a felt-tip pen or fine brush. The flower is not botanically precise; it is an artist's interpretation. Petals radiate outward with slightly irregular spacing and varying line weights, giving the impression that each stroke was drawn by hand in a single pass.

### Composition

The marigold illustration sits above or to the left of the studio name. The name "Marigold" is set in Caveat (the heading typeface) at a size that reads as hand-lettered rather than typeset. "Wellness Studio" appears below in Source Serif 4 at a smaller size, providing the professional anchor.

The overall composition is vertical-primary (flower above, text below) with a horizontal lockup available for navigation bars and tight spaces. The vertical version is the primary mark.

A standalone version of the hand-drawn marigold flower serves as the icon mark — used for favicons, social media avatars, and decorative repeating patterns.

### Symbolism

The marigold flower connects directly to the studio name, but the hand-drawn treatment transforms it from a literal botanical reference into a statement about the studio's character: this is a place where things are made with human hands and human attention. The imperfect line work communicates that Marigold Wellness Studio values authenticity over polish.

The single flower (not a bouquet, not a pattern) communicates focus and simplicity — one studio, one practice, one intention.

### Line Quality and Texture

The line work should have visible variation in weight — thicker where the pen pressed down, thinner at the turns. Small imperfections (a line that doesn't quite close, a petal slightly larger than its neighbor) are features, not flaws. The illustration should look like it was drawn on textured paper, even in digital form.

The logo should be provided in a clean single-color version (Charred Walnut on Parchment, or reversed) as well as a version with a subtle Saffron Ink wash filling the petals — as if watercolor were loosely applied inside the line drawing.

### Execution Notes for Designer

- Draw the marigold at 3-4x final size and reduce — this preserves hand-drawn imperfections at small sizes
- Do not auto-trace or vectorize aggressively; preserve the original stroke character
- The flower should have 12-16 visible petals with a textured center
- Test the standalone icon at 32px (favicon), 48px (social), and 120px+ (print) to ensure the hand-drawn quality reads at all sizes
- Avoid symmetry — the flower should feel grown, not constructed

## Imagery Direction

### Photography Style

**Mood:** Intimate, unhurried, imperfect. Photographs should feel like they were taken by someone who was present in the moment — not a photographer directing a scene. The camera is a quiet observer, not a participant.

**Lighting:** Natural light only. Side light and window light preferred. Soft, directional, with visible shadows that give dimension. Golden hour tones when outdoors. No flash, no studio lighting rigs, no artificially even illumination.

**Color Treatment:** Photographs should be edited to align with the brand palette. This means: slightly desaturated, warm mid-tones pulled toward saffron/amber, shadows pushed warm (toward brown, never blue), and highlights softened to avoid blown-out whites. The overall effect is as if the photograph were printed on the same Parchment-tone paper as the rest of the brand.

**Composition:** Slightly off-center framing. Close crops that emphasize texture — hands on a mat, the weave of a bolster, condensation on a water glass, a candle flame slightly out of focus. Wide shots should feel spacious but lived-in: a studio with props that aren't perfectly arranged, a shelf with objects at varying heights.

### Subject Matter

- Hands: hands in mudras, hands adjusting a bolster, hands wrapping a gift card, hands pouring tea. Hands are the primary subject of this brand's imagery because they represent the "handcrafted" concept literally.
- Textures: linen, raw wood, unglazed ceramic, dried flowers, beeswax, natural fiber rugs. These textures appear as backgrounds, detail shots, and decorative elements.
- Studio moments: a room before class starts (empty but warm), mats laid out with space between them, a sound bowl resting on a cushion. These should feel like the studio was photographed for a personal journal, not a magazine.
- People: when people appear, they are mid-practice or mid-conversation — never posed, never looking at the camera. Expressions are internal (eyes closed, gaze downward, soft smile to oneself). Diverse subjects in terms of age, body type, and skin tone — reflecting Sara's audience of women 30-55 who are health-conscious but not fitness-obsessed.

### Illustration Accents

Hand-drawn illustration accents extend the logo's line-art quality into the broader visual system:

- Small botanical sketches (marigold petals, leaves, seed pods) used as section dividers and decorative elements
- Hand-drawn underlines beneath key phrases or CTAs — irregular, brush-like strokes
- Rough-edge borders or frames around photographs, as if the photo were torn or hand-cut and placed into a scrapbook
- These illustration elements should be drawn in Charred Walnut or Saffron Ink, single-color, matching the logo's line weight and character

### Texture Overlays

Photographs and solid-color sections should occasionally carry a subtle paper texture overlay — the visual effect of content printed on handmade paper. This treatment is used sparingly (hero sections, feature callouts) to avoid becoming gimmicky. The texture should be barely perceptible at normal viewing distance but contribute to an overall feeling that the website is a crafted artifact rather than a digital product.

### Stock Imagery Guidelines

When selecting stock imagery, prioritize:
- Images shot in natural light with warm tones
- Lifestyle imagery in real spaces (not studios with white cyclorama walls)
- Close-up and detail shots over full-body or group poses
- Images with visible texture (wood grain, fabric weave, paper surface)
- Subjects who appear relaxed and unposed

### What to Avoid

Per Sara's anti-patterns from the intake record:

- **No stock photos of women in white rooms smiling at cameras.** This is the single most important avoidance. If a person is in the photo, they should not be aware of the camera.
- **No busy layouts.** Imagery should breathe. Generous whitespace (or "parchment-space") around every photograph. Never stack images edge-to-edge.
- **Nothing cold or corporate.** No blue-toned lighting, no glass-and-steel interiors, no images that could belong to a hospital or a corporate office.
- **No generic wellness tropes.** No smooth stones stacked by a stream. No single lotus flower on still water. No essential oil bottles artfully arranged on marble. These images signal mass-market wellness and undercut the handcrafted identity.
- **No digitally perfect imagery.** Overly retouched skin, HDR landscapes, or images with unnaturally saturated colors break the organic, hand-touched feeling of this direction.
